The LOGIN

Fans flew in from America, Australia and Aotearoa. Revus&rsquo; intro video — from gang culture to the stage — was the night&rsquo;s moment.

Aliyah Tautuhi-FraserFebruary 1, 20253 min read

More than just a concert, The LOGIN was an unforgettable celebration. With fans traveling from America, Australia, and Aotearoa, it was a gathering of people from all over, coming together to experience the talent of Polynesian artists. Flags waved proudly, music filled the air, and the crowd brought unmatched energy.

Featuring artists from America and Aotearoa, the event reflected the rich diaspora of Polynesian communities and highlighted how music serves as a bridge to reconnect with one another. With R&B and hip-hop dominating the lineup, the connection between Polynesian culture and these genres was evident. Both are deeply rooted in storytelling, cultural expression, and the blending of traditional and modern influences. This fusion has shaped a contemporary identity for Polynesian people, offering a way to navigate and affirm their place in multicultural societies. The duality of knowing how to move through both reflects in their music.

The crowd came ready to connect, and the sense of community was strong. Concerts like The LOGIN are not just about watching favorite artists perform; they’re about finding and celebrating a shared identity. Music has the power to tell stories, provoke emotions, and create a collective experience. It reflects who we are, where we come from, and where we’re going, uniting everyone who resonates with its messages.

The night was filled with surprises, including guest appearances by J KING and Shane Walker. It felt like a mini-festival, with artists supporting one another and showcasing the strong sense of community they’ve built within the industry. The love and connection shared on stage were mirrored by the crowd.

Highlight: Revus Performance Intro Video

A standout moment was Revus’s performance, introduced by a visionary video sharing his personal journey and connection to his Samoan heritage. Growing up as an urbanized Polynesian in New Zealand, Revus spoke about the struggles he faced growing up with humble beginnings and the pressures that led him to join gang culture. Through music, he found a way to rise above those circumstances, embrace his identity, and inspire others. His story resonated deeply with the audience, reflecting the shared experiences of many Polynesians navigating similar journeys.

Revus’s performance wasn’t just about him as an artist—it was about representing a collective experience. Whether through shared culture, upbringing, or aspirations, he reminded everyone that their stories matter and that they belong to a larger community. It was a moment of connection, empowerment, and celebration of what it means to be Polynesian.
