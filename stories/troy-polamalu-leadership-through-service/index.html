Troy Polamalu: Championing Leadership Through Service

The two-time Super Bowl champion on service, the Resilience Bowl's $200,000 night in Pittsburgh, and the rookie who grew up wearing his number.

Queenie Taua'aJune 1, 20244 min read

In the heart of Samoan wisdom lies a timeless proverb: “O le ala i le pule o le tautua” — meaning “the path to leadership is through service.” This saying resonates deeply with former Pittsburgh Steeler and two-time Super Bowl champion, Troy Polamalu. While his gridiron achievements are celebrated, Troy treasures his role as a dedicated humanitarian above all else.

The Inaugural Resilience Bowl

Returning to Pittsburgh alongside his wife, Theodora, they proudly led the inaugural Resilience Bowl, a fundraising event benefiting the Neighborhood Resilience Project. This local initiative, rooted in the Hill District, provides crucial support to underserved communities grappling with racial inequity and crime. The Polamalus are staunch supporters of this cause, embodying the spirit of service in action.

The Resilience Bowl showcased not only spirited competition but also a gathering of stars committed to making a difference. As dusk settled, cheers erupted for Troy and fellow celebrities, including rappers Fedd and Chevy Woods, along with beloved Steelers alumni such as James Harrison, Chris Hope, Ike Taylor, Ben Roethlisberger, and Ryan Clark. Even Steelers head coach Mike Tomlin joined in as referee, adding to the excitement of the evening.

For Troy, the event held meaningful significance. Reuniting with former teammates underscored the bonds forged on and off the field, all in support of a worthy cause. By night's end, over $200,000 had been raised, a testament to the community's generosity and commitment to resilience. During the press conference, our teammate Serafina Maulupe posed a question to Troy: “Being Samoan and coming from a culture where we are naturally givers, how does it feel to give back to a community that has given so much to you?”

In a reflective moment at the Resilience Bowl, Troy shared his thoughts on giving back to the community that shaped him. For him, it all begins with a sense of ownership and familial bonds, echoing the ethos of the Rooney family and the Steelers organization. This sense of kinship, he believes, is the cornerstone of success, both on and off the field.

During his induction into the 2021 Pro Football Hall of Fame, Troy shared insights into his roots, emphasizing the influence of his Samoan heritage. He expressed how values like discipline, humility, and respect form the bedrock of Samoan culture, guiding not only survival but also existence. As a first-generation American-Samoan, Troy takes pride in representing his family's lineage, bridging the journey from American Samoa to the NFL.

Around the globe, Troy Polamalu is hailed as a legend, not only for his athletic talent but also for his enduring legacy of service and compassion. His story serves as a beacon of hope, reminding us that true greatness is measured not only by touchdowns but also by the impact we have on others. As his legacy continues to inspire, Troy Polamalu remains the epitome of greatness, both in the game of football and in the game of life.

Double the Troy, Double the Steel

A Steelers' full-circle moment: did you catch that Troy Fautanu, the offensive lineman from UW who was picked by the Pittsburgh Steelers in the 20th spot of the 2024 NFL draft's 1st round, actually grew up as a hardcore fan of Troy Polamalu and the Steelers? Talk about a full-circle moment, huh? Throughout his life, he sported the number 43 in honor of Polamalu until his switch to the offensive line, which unfortunately meant he had to let go of that number.

“Growing up, just being a big fan of Troy and being a big fan of the Steelers, I grew to love the city, grew to love the team, and it couldn't have been written any better than this.”

We caught up with Fautanu at the Resilience Bowl event hosted by Troy and Theodora. He expressed his desire to emulate Troy's path and give back to his hometown of Vegas someday. “I'm just grateful to be here. Getting the opportunity to see what a guy like him has done for the community inspires me to do something back home in Vegas, and I am super excited to learn from him in that way.”

This marks the beginning of the Troy x Troy legacy, and it's incredible witnessing this dream come true for Fautanu; it seems like destiny had a hand in it all along. Now, Fautanu gets to play for the team he grew up rooting for and can look to Polamalu as his mentor. It's a past-meets-present legacy in the making for both Troys.
