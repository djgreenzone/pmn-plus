JuJu Smith-Schuster: Fire Within

The Super Bowl champion on AFI, the Samoan word for fire, a household of 23, the ‘ie faitaga on game day, and the flame that burns beyond football.

Queenie Taua'aJanuary 1, 20269 min read

We all know JuJu. JuJu Smith-Schuster. But knowing JuJu and understanding JuJu are two very different things.

From the moment he stepped onto an NFL field, JuJu Smith-Schuster has played the game with a joy that feels almost rebellious in a league built on brutality. Yes, he brings physicality as a wide receiver, but what truly sets him apart is his spirit. His personality. His confidence. His unmistakable cultural presence. JuJu never just plays football. He shows up as himself.

Born in Long Beach, California, JuJu was raised in a home grounded in family, resilience, and discipline. Football was never simply a game. It was an outlet, a responsibility, and eventually, a platform. At USC, he became known for his toughness and versatility, earning a reputation as a receiver willing to do the dirty work: blocking, battling through contact, and delivering in the moments that mattered most.

Drafted by the Pittsburgh Steelers in 2017, JuJu made his mark quickly. He refused to shrink himself in a sport that often demands conformity. His infectious energy, social media presence, and signature celebrations signaled the arrival of a new generation of athletes, ones who embraced individuality without sacrificing work ethic. Behind the highlights and headlines was a young man learning, evolving, and growing up under the brightest spotlight in professional sports.

In 2023, JuJu added a Super Bowl ring to his résumé with the Kansas City Chiefs, cementing his legacy not just as a playmaker, but as a proven contributor on championship teams. His career has been defined by transition, new teams, new roles, new expectations, and through each chapter, JuJu has met the moment with adaptability and humility.

Off the field, that same intentionality remains. JuJu is vocal about gratitude, family, and staying true to himself. He uses his platform not to chase perfection, but growth, to inspire younger athletes who see themselves in him.

JuJu Smith-Schuster’s story isn’t just about football. It’s about identity. It’s about resilience. It’s about redefining what it means to be an athlete in today’s era. Loud when he needs to be, reflective when it matters most, and always authentic. JuJu continues to write his story on his own terms.

AFI [Fire]: The Flame Beyond Football

If you didn’t know, JuJu Smith-Schuster is also an entrepreneur, and this is the chapter we wanted to spotlight. Our team had the opportunity to sit down with JuJu himself to talk about his clothing brand, AFI [FIRE]. Afi means fire in the Samoan language.

JuJu shared that his mom is Samoan and that he grew up in a Polynesian household. He is afakasi, half Black, and openly shared that his biological father wasn’t really in his life. Instead, it was his Samoan stepfather who stepped in. JuJu also shared that he has four sisters and two brothers, laughing as he said, “as most polys know, it’s normal to have a big family.”

Family and culture were always central to his upbringing. JuJu explained that growing up, “we had about 23 people in the household. Me and my immediate family stayed in the garage, but it felt like we had koaga’i every night with the whole family.” That experience shaped how he views everything he creates today.

When he first started his clothing brand, JuJu shared that he wanted it to represent more than football. “When people think about JuJu Smith-Schuster, I didn’t want it to be just football. I wanted them to think about everything else I have going on too.”

AFI, for JuJu, represents the fire within. He explained that fire is the passion that drives him, football being one of them. “Football was my fire, my spark. It’s what got me to where I am today.” But AFI was created to show that expression doesn’t have to live in just one space.

Through the brand, JuJu wanted to encourage people to express themselves beyond what they’re known for. AFI allows him to blend family, culture, and passion into something tangible. His first collection dropped about two years ago, and he’s now preparing to release his third collection at the beginning of this year. He also shared his excitement about an upcoming collaboration with Utah-based Polynesian artist Fred Frost.

Fashion as Self-Expression

So how did fashion become part of JuJu’s journey? It started with simply being himself.

JuJu shared that his personality has always been big, and his style reflects that. Whether it’s bright colors, baggy jeans, or jorts, fashion has always been his way of expressing who he is. For him, it’s not about trends, it’s about authenticity.

He explained that every game day outfit is intentional. “For every game, I wear something I love, something that really shows my personality.” Being able to translate that same personality into clothing people can wear has been especially meaningful.

“When you’re able to dress how you want, and then also create something people can wear too, something they can be part of, that’s what makes it really cool.”

AFI is growing slowly and intentionally. JuJu shared that it’s about consistency, building it day by day and continuing to push forward.

“It’s just cool, man. Being able to step out of my comfort zone in the fashion world has been fun, and being able to collaborate with other Polynesians makes it even cooler.”

Culture, Responsibility, and Representation

“The responsibility is a lot, it’s huge. It’s a big statement. I’m proud to show that I grew up in a household where we had White Sunday, we went to loku kamaiki, and you had to say your Bible verse. For me, being able to express our culture through clothing is a huge statement.”

JuJu shared this when we asked about showcasing Samoan culture through his pre-game outfits, specifically when he wore the traditional ‘ie faitaga, a Samoan and Polynesian men’s garment, ahead of NFL game days.

That visibility mattered.

He shared that once he began wearing traditional Samoan attire, many of his Polynesian brothers reached out to him, expressing pride and appreciation. “A lot of my Polynesian boys would hit me up and tell me I was putting on for the culture and that they were proud.” He also shared that many people didn’t even know he was Polynesian until he made that statement on such a large stage. “It just showed that it’s bigger than football, and I’m not afraid to express that side of myself.”

Roots That Shape the Man

Reflecting on his upbringing, JuJu laughed and said, “of course getting fasi’d was normal.” But beyond the humor, the lessons ran deep.

“One thing I hold dearly from growing up is always putting God first, respecting your elders, and treating people the way you want to be treated. Loving others, that’s what made me who I am today. It taught me how to be humble and thankful for everything I have and everything God has given me.”

Faith remains central to his life. JuJu shared that in a world that’s easy to get caught up in, grounding yourself in gratitude is essential. Giving thanks to God, he said, is something that always keeps him centered.

At the heart of it all is family, love, and togetherness.

Learning From Greatness

Respecting elders has also shaped JuJu’s professional journey, especially in the quarterbacks he’s had the opportunity to work with.

Entering the league at just 20 years old, JuJu joined the Pittsburgh Steelers alongside Ben Roethlisberger, who was nearing the end of his career. JuJu shared that he had to adapt quickly, learning how to adjust his game and absorb everything around him. He described Big Ben as a leader who led by example, something JuJu deeply related to, as it mirrored how his stepfather raised him.

On the other hand, playing with Patrick Mahomes brought a different energy. JuJu shared that Mahomes is also a great leader, but one closer to him in age. “It’s always fun playing with him,” JuJu said, describing Mahomes as a true playmaker.

The biggest difference, he explained, was timing, learning from Big Ben at a veteran stage while growing alongside Patrick. Both experiences, JuJu shared, have played a major role in shaping his career.

A Super Bowl Moment and a Family Victory

JuJu Smith-Schuster won his Super Bowl championship with the Kansas City Chiefs in Super Bowl LVII, recording seven catches for 53 yards in the victory over the Philadelphia Eagles.

For JuJu, it was about more than football.

He shared how meaningful it was to be wanted by a program, to be brought in, embraced, and trusted by the coaches and players. The 2022–23 season tested him through adversity and injury, but he kept his faith strong and continued to work.

That perseverance paid off.

One of the most special moments was bringing his family to the Super Bowl, nearly 60 family members in attendance. “You know how Polys are, we stick together and we roll deep.”

After the game, JuJu stood with the Samoan flag draped over his shoulders, wearing his ula, celebrating not just a championship but everyone who helped him get there.

Entrepreneurship and Advice for the Next Generation

When it comes to entrepreneurship, JuJu shared that he plans to apply everything he’s learned on the field, discipline, preparation, and consistency, to building AFI.

When asked about financial advice for the young Polynesian community, his response was honest and necessary.

JuJu shared that while our culture is rooted in generosity, there also has to be balance and boundaries. “Our hearts are big, we love to give, but there has to be value and limits too.”

He explained that many young people don’t always see the full picture, taxes, rent, responsibilities, and that financial literacy is essential. “We love our family, but there’s a right way to go about it and a smart way to take care of your family financially. That’s one of the biggest things I want to pass on to the young ones.”
