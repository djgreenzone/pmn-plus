Samuelu Elisaia's Journey with Faith and the FCA

The coach's kid from Leone on identity, the cold-tub meeting that changed his life, and the FCA camp he is working to build in the Pacific Islands.

Keilani MayoDecember 1, 20255 min read

In the dynamic world of college athletics, where identity often becomes intertwined with performance, Samuelu Elisaia expresses a change in narrative. A proud Samoan, a coach's kid, and a leader in the Fellowship of Christian Athletes (FCA), Elisaia's life is a testament to rooting one's identity in something irrevocable: faith! Something us Pacific Islanders know all too well.

The Coach's Kid Finds His Roots

As some army brats might relate, coach's kids also have their fair share in moving around the country. Samuelu Elisaia, 27, grew up constantly on the move across the Midwest — from Iowa to Kansas, Michigan, and Kentucky — as his father pursued a coaching career. Born in Iowa, Elisaia's early life was dominated by the culture of sports. It wasn't until his family settled in Utah at the age of nine, where his father became the strength coach at the University of Utah. Go Utes!

It was him finally being settled in Utah where Elisaia was first immersed and introduced to a large Polynesian community. This move sparked a deep connection to his Samoan heritage from the village of Leone in American Samoa.

Growing up under the shadow of a successful coach, Elisaia felt the pressure to perform, especially when he played for his father at the University of Utah. Yet, his father's approach fostered a love for football and a strong personal work ethic.

1 Corinthians 2:9

The most pivotal moment in Elisaia's journey came not on the field, but off it. During his senior year of high school, finally being granted the freedom to choose whether or not to attend church, he realized his faith was merely a tradition inherited from his parents. This realization led to a period of questioning whether or not this thing called “faith” is real. I know we can all relate to that!

The crisis of identity deepened during his freshman year at a local junior college. Facing a difficult transition in football and a personal breakup, Elisaia felt his identity “slip away.” It was a call from his father, urging him to “get right with God,” that prompted him to rededicate his life to Christ.

After transferring to Utah, Elisaia was actively praying for positive influences. His answer came unexpectedly in the cold tub, where he met a teammate who was also the FCA leader on campus. Feeling led by the spirit, his teammate invited Elisaia to a meeting. What started as a small huddle, with only about eight attendees, has grown, drawing 50 to 60 athletes throughout campus. Elisaia's involvement blossomed into a career working for the FCA, where he now guides others through the same challenges he overcame.

Elisaia's core message, especially for Pacific Islander athletes, is heavy on identity! He emphasizes that before being Polynesian, Samoan, Tongan, or Fijian, they are a child of God.

“Your identity should be rooted in Christ, not something that is ‘up and down' like sports performance.”

He drives this point home by citing the example of Raiders running back Ashton Jeanty, who famously described himself as “a Christian cleverly disguised as a Heisman running back.” For Elisaia, prioritizing faith allows athletes to be freed from the burden of performance, ultimately leading to better results. He shares a personal testimony that when he truly began to pursue the Lord during his senior year, his athletic performance improved because his self-worth was no longer tied to his statistics.

As Pacific Islanders, we understand our culture is so deeply rooted in who we are, but at the same time faith is also just as important. I asked Elisaia about balancing the rich Pacific Islander culture with a relationship with the Lord and expounding on the challenges of preventing culture from becoming an idol.

His advice is practical: spend time in the Word (the Bible), memorize scripture, and ask the hard questions about one's beliefs. Understanding God's love and authority, as revealed in the Bible, is the key to removing idols and establishing God at the center.

Looking ahead, Elisaia has immense love and hope for the next generation of Pacific Islanders in faith. He believes the culture has much to offer in the faith conversation, including wisdom and a unique spirit of joy where Pacific Islanders just naturally know how to “have fun.”

In an exciting new venture, Elisaia is working to establish an FCA camp in the Pacific Islands. With the Pacific Islands named the international partner for the FCA Rockies region, he is involved in organizing a “vision trip” with a multi-year plan to build a camp that blends sports and faith, bridging a crucial gap for Pacific Islander athletes.

From a coach's kid bouncing around the Midwest to a committed leader facilitating and stewarding a generation of athletes in Christ, Samuelu Elisaia's journey is a powerful reminder that true purpose and excellence emerge when one's identity is found not in what they do, but in who they are in God.
