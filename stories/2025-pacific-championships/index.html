Pacific Pride: The 2025 Pacific Championships

Record crowds, 1.12 million viewers for the final, and a tournament that moved Pacific rugby league from the margins to the centre — with RLWC2026 next.

Queenie Taua'aDecember 1, 20256 min read

The 2025 Pacific Championships were not just another international rugby league tournament. They became a watershed moment for the game's cultural and commercial rise across the Pacific. From sell out stadiums to record breaking broadcasts, this year's edition proved that the Pacific community has arrived and they are making their voice heard.

Attendance, Atmosphere & Historic Crowds

  • The 2025 Pacific Championships recorded the highest attendance of any edition ever staged, with record setting crowds across Australia, New Zealand, and Papua New Guinea. (nrl.com)
  • Key matches were packed out. Venues like Suncorp Stadium in Brisbane, Eden Park in Auckland, and CommBank Stadium in Sydney hosted massive turnouts, showing that fans are hungry for Pacific rugby league at its highest level.
  • Every hosting city contributed to a total crowd tally that reflected enormous regional appetite for the sport. The atmosphere was charged, pulsing with pride, identity, and community support. For many fans, the Pacific Championships was not just about sport but about culture, representation, and belonging.

Viewership — A New Benchmark for Pacific Interest

  • The fixture between Samoa and Tonga became the most watched match between those two sides ever, drawing 650,000 viewers across Australia and New Zealand.
  • The Final between New Zealand and Samoa smashed records as the most watched men's Pacific Championships game of all time, with 1.12 million viewers tuning in across Australia and New Zealand.
  • These numbers show that whole new swathes of the Pacific community see these games as must watch events, not marginal footnotes between club seasons.

Even in markets where traditional powerhouses such as the Australia Kangaroos were not playing, viewership rose. This shows that Pacific fixtures themselves now command serious attention.

Rising Engagement Even Without the Kangaroos

The 2025 edition did not include matches involving the traditional powerhouse Australia Kangaroos. Despite this, several major Australian markets still grew. Melbourne was up 9.7 percent, Perth 2.4 percent, and Western Australia 50 percent in cumulative viewership year on year. That is a rare feat for a tournament missing one of its biggest draws.

For context, typical rugby union Tests such as those between the Australian national rugby union side and touring teams average around 800,000 viewers in Australia, and even marquee contests like the first match of the Bledisloe Cup rate roughly 440,000. The 2025 Pacific Championships Final's 1.12 million easily exceeded these benchmarks.

This surge shows that the Pacific Championships are not just supplementing traditional fixtures. They are emerging as a major standalone sporting event with broad cross cultural appeal.

What It Means: A Turning Point for Rugby League & the Pacific Narrative

The success of the 2025 Pacific Championships goes beyond trophies and scores. It signals a shift in the sport's identity and audience. Where once international rugby league, particularly Pacific match ups, occupied niche corners of the calendar, they now sit at the center.

For Pacific Island communities and their diasporas, the tournament offered more than sport. It provided visibility, unity, and representation. Stadium flags, chants in native languages, families reuniting around ancestral pride.

From a broader industry standpoint, strong attendance, surging viewership, and commercial momentum show that Pacific rugby league is not just viable. It is vital.

For clubs, broadcasters, leagues, and the communities who bleed blue, red, green, or whatever color they wear, the message is clear: the Pacific is here.

RLWC2026: The Stage Is Set and the Hype Is Real

With the echoes of 2025 still ringing loud, attention now shifts to RLWC2026, the most anticipated international rugby league event in recent memory. (nrl.com)

  • RLWC2026 will be hosted across Australia, Papua New Guinea, and New Zealand, making it a truly Pacific wide celebration of rugby league. (en.wikipedia.org)
  • The tournament will feature Men's, Women's, and Wheelchair competitions with 26 teams in total, expanding representation and showcasing the sport's growing inclusivity and global reach. (rugby-league.com)
  • The grand finals for both Men's and Women's competitions will be held at Suncorp Stadium in Brisbane, a fitting venue after its own recent sell out success during the Pacific Championships. (rlwc2026.com)
  • Organisers promise a high octane, compact, must watch tournament. Every match will be competitive and every game meaningful. For fans, this is not just about national pride. It is about being part of history. (nrl.com)

RLWC2026 arrives at a moment when Pacific rugby league has never been stronger and global attention has never been more focused.

What to Expect: For Pacific Islanders, Diaspora, and Rugby League Fans

  • A chance to see Pacific talent shine on the world's biggest stage, whether you are cheering for Samoa, Tonga, Fiji, PNG, or another island nation.
  • A rare mix of elite competition and cultural celebration. RLWC2026 is not just about the scoreboard, it is also about heritage, identity, and unity.
  • Fans in Australia, New Zealand, PNG, and beyond will witness world class matches in stadiums across the region, offering real opportunities to gather, celebrate, and connect.
  • Women and differently abled athletes will take center stage. The inclusion of Women's and Wheelchair tournaments signals a broader, more inclusive future for rugby league, one where the sport belongs to everyone.

What to Watch and What Is at Stake?

  • Will teams from the Pacific continue their upward trajectory and challenge traditional heavyweights?
  • Can RLWC2026 build on the momentum from 2025 to create a global surge in rugby league popularity, especially among younger, diaspora, and under-represented communities?
  • Most importantly, can the sense of culture, pride, and unity that defined the 2025 Pacific Championships carry through
