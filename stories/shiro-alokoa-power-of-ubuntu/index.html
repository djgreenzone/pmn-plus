I Am Because You Are: Shiro Alokoa and the Power of Ubuntu

The Kosraean-Hawaii raised dancer on Island Breeze IWT, putting his music on pause, and making room for Micronesian voices.

Queenie Taua'aApril 1, 20254 min read

In a world often divided by borders and backgrounds, art and faith have the extraordinary ability to weave people together. For Shiro Alokoa, a young man with roots in Kosrae, Micronesia, and a life shaped in Hawaii, this connection is not just a concept, it's his reality. His journey with Island Breeze IWT, a ministry that breathes life into faith through dance and music, is a testament to the unifying power of culture and spirituality.

When we sat down with Shiro, what began as a simple conversation quickly became an intimate look into his heart, a story of identity, purpose, and the transformative force of art. More than a ministry, his path is about reclaiming heritage, fostering unity, and stepping into a calling far greater than himself.

A Path Unfolds

Shiro's journey to ministry was anything but conventional. "I grew up Christian," he shared, "but I didn't know I could have a relationship with the Lord until I found out why I was in Kona." That realization led him to a Discipleship Training School (DTS), where his perspective shifted from simply believing in faith to actively living it.

His introduction to Island Breeze IWT happened by chance, but the pull was undeniable. He watched his cousin Bless and the team perform at church, mesmerized by how they "used culture to share the gospel." The idea that hula, music, and movement could be a vessel for faith struck a chord deep within him. "I didn't know we could use music and different gifts and talents to share the gospel," he said, his voice carrying the weight of newfound purpose.

Island Breeze IWT, founded in American Samoa in 1979, has spent decades traveling the world, using dance and music to spread hope. Their mission, what Shiro calls "a movement where we use the dances to redeem the culture," resonates deeply with him. For too long, he has seen Micronesian voices overshadowed in larger Polynesian narratives. Now, he sees an opportunity to change that. "We love to sing," he said. "That's something we grew up with… and something I can use to help get our people out there."

Balancing Passion and Purpose

Like many artists walking the tightrope between creative dreams and spiritual callings, Shiro has had to make sacrifices. His music, something he once pursued with passion, is currently on pause. But to him, it's not a loss, it's a matter of priority. "Right now, my main focus is to bring our people to the word," he explained. "If you put God first, everything else will work around that."

That focus is evident in his commitment to an upcoming trip to Samoa and American Samoa, where Island Breeze IWT will celebrate its 40th-50th anniversary. The team will be working with We Samoa, a local organization dedicated to community service. Their mission extends beyond performance, it's about reaching the youth, tackling issues like drug use, and reminding the next generation that faith offers a path forward. "I just want them to know that God has bigger plans than whatever they think they have," Shiro said.

The Power of Ubuntu

More than anything, Shiro believes in unity. He embraces the philosophy of Ubuntu, "I am because you are." To him, this means recognizing that we are all connected, that every culture, every voice, and every story matters. Through his ministry, he hopes to inspire people to "dive deep into their culture" and embrace their identity with pride.

But the road of a missionary is not easy. Island Breeze IWT is self-funded, meaning constant fundraising and personal sacrifice. Shiro has missed family events, set aside personal aspirations, and faced the challenges that come with choosing a life of service. Yet, every obstacle has only strengthened his faith, deepening his trust in God's plan.

For those who feel called to support or be part of Island Breeze IWT, Shiro extends an open invitation: reach out through their Instagram page, @IslandBreezeIWT.

As our conversation came to an end, Shiro reflected on the legacy he hopes to leave behind: "Redemption, reconciliation, and working in unity and diversity." In a world often divided, his story serves as a beacon of hope, a reminder that faith, culture, and art can bring us together, creating something far greater than ourselves.
