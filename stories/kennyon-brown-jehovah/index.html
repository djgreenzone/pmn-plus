Kennyon Brown and the Message Behind “Jehovah”

The Samoan artist on the wake-up call that brought him back to God, and the video he shot on the fly in Darwin.

Queenie Taua'aMay 1, 20244 min read

Kennyon Brown has just released his first song of the year, titled “Jehovah,” and he's graciously opened up about the inspiration and message behind it. Reflecting on a challenging period in his life, Kennyon shares how he felt a bit lost and out of sync. It was a wake-up call from someone close that prompted him to reassess his situation. He realized he had lacked his relationship with God, a connection deeply ingrained in his upbringing as a member of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.

“How perfect can you be, when you owe nothing to me? And still love me unconditionally.”

In a moment of clarity, Kennyon shed tears not of sadness, but of relief, as he felt God's presence helping him overcome his struggles. Within hours, he penned the lyrics to “Jehovah,” the first verse and chorus flowing effortlessly as if they were a gift from above. Each word felt like a direct message from God, guiding him through his creative process.

“You can go wherever you want in life and do whatever you want, but there will come a time when reality will hit you, and without God centered in your life, it's going to be hard.”

That is his message behind the song.

Kennyon's “Jehovah” isn't just a song; it's a heartfelt message. The accompanying visual piece, directed by Dream, adds another layer of power and impact. Shot in Darwin, Australia, it wasn't originally planned as such. Kennyon was there for “Project Love,” but with the blessing of the community's traditional owners, he and Dream decided to expand the project. With no set plan, they embraced spontaneity. Kennyon, accustomed to detailed planning, felt nervous. Yet, he prayed for guidance, and the result exceeded his expectations. Completed in January, the video showcases stunning scenes from his chapel, the Blue Mountains, and his home. Kennyon cherishes the opening conversation with his father, a man of faith. His parents always stress the importance of a strong relationship with God. Kennyon records these conversations, treasuring them as his parents age, ensuring their wisdom and love remain a timeless part of his life.

Kennyon wants people to know that despite not having a good history, he believes in the power of God to bring salvation and positive change. He's now committed to improving himself and walking a path closer to God. He admits he won't be perfect, but he's still passionate about making music, even if it evolves over time. This change in his life reflects a deeper spiritual journey he's embarked on.

“I want to inspire younger musicians and those who look up to me, showing them that it is okay to fail or to not be perfect. At some point in life, you can turn to God and make yourself better and become a better person.”
